Set up the push

Force along the motion, Fₓ vs. x

The shaded area under the curve is the work done by the applied force. For a constant force it is a rectangle: Fₓ · x.

Where the energy goes

Start with the kinetic energy you had, add the work the push does, subtract what friction takes. What is left is the kinetic energy now.

Work ledger, force by force

Gravity and the normal force are perpendicular to the motion, so they do zero work. Add up the work from every force and you get the change in kinetic energy.

Predict first, then check

Set up a push above. Before you press Play, use work to estimate the speed at the end of the run.

Try these

  1. Double the distance with a constant force. What happens to the work, and to the speed? (Speed grows with √W, not W.)
  2. Tilt the force to 60°. The push is still F, so why is the final speed smaller?
  3. Turn on friction until the block barely speeds up. Find where the two shaded areas on the graph match.
  4. Set the force to 0, give the block a starting speed, and turn on friction. Is the work by friction positive or negative? What does the waterfall show?
  5. Choose a force that grows linearly. Estimate the work from the triangle, then check it against the ledger. In calculus form this is W = ∫ Fₓ dx.
