The big picture

Energy is the "currency" of physics. Work is how a force deposits or withdraws energy, the energy is stored as kinetic (motion) or potential (arrangement) energy, it's conserved unless something outside the system takes it, and power is how fast it all happens.

3.1 Translational Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because it's moving. It's a scalar, it depends on the square of the speed, and its value depends on who is watching.

1 Key ideas

The definition

K = ½mv2

Units: kg·m²/s² = joules (J). Mass and speed are all you need.

Scalar, never negative

Squaring the speed wipes out direction. A car going 20 m/s east has the same K as one going 20 m/s west. K ≥ 0 always.

Speed squared matters

Double the speed → 4× the kinetic energy. Triple it → 9×. Halve it → ¼.

Depends on reference frame

Use the speed relative to the observer. Moving with an object makes it seem slower (less K); moving toward it makes it seem faster (more K).

2 Explore

⚠️ Watch out for…

  • Saying K is negative because the velocity is negative. Velocity has a sign; kinetic energy does not.
  • Saying "the velocity changed direction, so K changed." Only the speed matters.
  • Reading ΔK from a graph using the whole path — ΔK only needs the initial and final speeds.
  • Forgetting the observer: from a truck going 15 m/s, a car going 25 m/s moves at only 10 m/s.

3.2 Work

Work is energy transferred into or out of a system by a force acting over a displacement. Only the part of the force along the displacement counts.

1 Key ideas

Definition

W = F∥d = Fd cos θ

θ is the angle between the force and the displacement.

Sign of work

+ if the force has a component with the motion (θ < 90°), 0 if perpendicular (θ = 90°, like tension in a swinging pendulum or the normal force on a level floor), − if against the motion (friction, a fan blowing backward).

Area under F vs. x

When the force changes, work = the area between the force–position graph and the axis. Break it into rectangles and triangles.

Work–energy theorem

Wnet = ΔK

Add up the work done by every force. Net work is nonzero only when the kinetic energy is changing (speeding up or slowing down).

Work done by gravity

Wg = −mgΔy. Only vertical displacement matters — the path length up a ramp doesn't.

2 Explore

⚠️ Watch out for…

  • Thinking every force does work. Forces perpendicular to motion (tension in a circle, normal force on a flat surface) do zero work.
  • Using the ramp length for gravity's work. Use the height change.
  • Using sin instead of cos: W = Fd cos θ when θ is measured from the displacement.
  • Confusing "inversely proportional" (a curve) with "decreasing linearly" (a straight line).

3.3 Potential Energy

Potential energy belongs to a system of interacting objects and depends on their arrangement (configuration). You need at least two objects — e.g., the object and Earth, or the block and spring.

1 Key ideas

Gravity near Earth

ΔUg = mgΔy

Δy is the change in height of the center of mass. Only changes are physical — you choose where U = 0.

Springs

Us = ½k(Δx)2

Δx = how far the spring is from its relaxed length — stretched or compressed. Zero only at the relaxed length.

Gravity, any distance

UG = −Gm1m2r

Zero at infinite separation, negative everywhere else. Farther apart → U increases (toward zero). U ∝ 1/r, not 1/r2.

2 Explore

⚠️ Watch out for…

  • Measuring height to the top or bottom of an object instead of its center of mass.
  • Thinking a spring has no energy when compressed. It has energy whenever it's not at its relaxed length.
  • Thinking equal extra compressions store equal energy. Because Us ∝ x2, each extra step stores more.
  • Saying UG decreases as an orbit gets bigger. It's negative, so getting closer to zero is an increase.
  • Using 1/r2 for energy ratios — that's the force law.

3.4 Conservation of Energy

The total energy of a system changes only if something outside the system does work on it. So the first step is always: what's in my system?

1 Key ideas

Mechanical energy

Emech = K + Ug + Us

Count only the forms of energy that exist inside your chosen system, all at the same instant.

Closed system → constant

If no external forces do work (and nothing dissipates energy), Ki + Ui = Kf + Uf. Energy changes form; the total doesn't.

Friction & drag dissipate

Friction from a surface (or air resistance) is external to an object–Earth system and turns mechanical energy into thermal energy: Ef = Ei − Ffd

System choice changes the story

Include Earth → gravity is internal and stores Ug. Leave Earth out → gravity is an external force doing work, and there is no Ug. Same physics, different bookkeeping.

2 Explore

⚠️ Watch out for…

  • "Energy is always conserved, so mechanical energy stays the same." Total energy of the universe is conserved — mechanical energy of a system is not if friction or drag acts.
  • Including mgh when Earth isn't in the system.
  • Adding K from one instant to U from another instant (double-counting).
  • Constant speed ≠ constant mechanical energy: a skydiver at terminal speed loses Ug with no gain in K.
  • On a ramp, friction is μkmg cos θ, not μkmg.

3.5 Power

Power is the rate of energy transfer — how fast work is done. Unit: the watt (1 W = 1 J/s).

1 Key ideas

Average power

Pavg = WΔt = ΔEΔt

Same work in half the time → twice the power.

Instantaneous power

Pinst = F∥v

Force component along the velocity × speed at that instant. Same force, faster → more power.

Area under P vs. t

ΔE = area under a power–time graph (just like work = area under F vs. x). The slope doesn't give energy.

2 Explore

⚠️ Watch out for…

  • Thinking a longer ramp takes more power. Instantaneous power at constant speed depends on the ramp's angle, not its length.
  • Picking the largest force (or shortest time) in a data table without computing Fd/Δt.
  • Using the slope of a P–t graph for energy — use the area.
  • Unit check: power ends in kg·m²/s³. mv/t is a force, not a power.
